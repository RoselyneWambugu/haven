Echo — Wireframes: Returning-user login and account security

Draft v0.1 · low-fidelity · follows PRD §6.1 (ACC-11 and the new ACC-13 to ACC-21) and decision D35 (phone + one-time code, no passwords). Registration is screens 27–28; account standing and appeals are screen 31. Placeholders are marked ___ . Pink markers match the numbered notes beside each group. Not committed.

Principles

  • Urgent help never needs a login. The orange “Need urgent help?” pill is on every sign-in screen.
  • No passwords to forget, reuse or be shoulder-surfed. A phone code, a device, and a lock on the app.
  • Safe on a shared phone. Pen name hidden on the lock screen by default; “shared device” mode; notifications that say nothing private; quick exit.
  • No account enumeration. Screens never confirm whether a phone number has an account.
  • Recovery is humane and safe: a locked account is never deleted; a person reviews ID checks; every access is logged.

Decisions and gaps

  • D41: shared-device behaviour: session length (24 h drawn), what is cleared from the phone, and whether it can be the default for some members.
  • D42: recovery for a lost phone: which proofs are accepted, who reviews, how long it takes, and how to stop an attacker using the recovery route.
  • D43: discreet features: quick exit, neutral notification text, and whether Echo can offer a neutral app name or icon on Android.
  • D44: new-device alerts by SMS as well as in the app, and the cost of those SMS messages.
  • Biometrics: app lock uses the phone’s own fingerprint or PIN; Echo never stores biometric data.
  • Not drawn: the support-side screen where staff review a recovery request (admin), and web sign-in for therapists and staff (they use MFA, PRD RBAC-2).
